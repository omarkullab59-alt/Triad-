The mill had been closed for eleven years. The looms were gone, sold for scrap, but the floor still carried their outline — rectangles of cleaner concrete where the machines once stood, like shadows that had forgotten to leave.

We brought nine looks and one rule: nothing could be pressed. Every coat arrived the way it had travelled, creased, folded, carrying the shape of the bag it came in. Wool remembers. That was the whole idea.

## Cloth as armour

Winter dressing is usually sold as comfort. Here it is closer to defence. Boiled wool cut long and stiff, shoulders that hold their line without padding, collars raised until the face becomes an argument between fabric and skin.

> The coat is not there to keep you warm. It is there to keep you. — Mara Solenne, stylist

The stylist pulled most of the pieces from two sources: an emerging London label working exclusively with military surplus, and a box of unlabelled garments found at a Brussels flea market. Nobody knows who made them. That is the point of an archive — some voices only survive as objects.

![A figure in a long wool coat against a concrete wall](https://picsum.photos/seed/triad-wool-fig1/1800/1200?grayscale "Look 04. Boiled wool coat, surplus trousers, no name.")

## Three hours of light

The mill faced north. We had three hours of usable light, and Ines refused to bring a single lamp. Every frame was shot on expired film, pushed two stops, developed in a bathroom in Neukölln the same night. The grain is not an effect. It is evidence of the conditions.

What emerged is less a fashion story than a study in endurance. Bodies standing very still in rooms that were built for movement. Garments doing the work of architecture.
