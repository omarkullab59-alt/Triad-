The brief was one sentence: make something for a room you will never see. Three artists — a designer, a sound artist and a photographer — each received the same sentence and the same deadline. They were not allowed to speak to each other.

## Room I — Form

Lior Baptiste cast six garments in plaster and suspended them at head height. Visitors had to walk beneath them, close enough to see the fabric texture preserved in the white surface.

## Room II — Signal

Tomás Reyna recorded the sound of each garment being made: scissors, needle, iron, the hiss of steam. He slowed the recordings until they sounded like weather.

> I wanted the clothes to be heard before they were seen. — Tomás Reyna

## Room III — Image

Ines Varga photographed empty chairs in the homes of people who had worn the same coat for more than ten years. No people, only the indentation they left.

![Plaster garments suspended in a white room](https://picsum.photos/seed/triad-rooms-fig1/1800/1200?grayscale "Room I. Plaster, cotton, wire.")

---

Shown together, the three rooms produced something none of the artists planned: a single piece about absence, about the body as something garments remember. That is what TRIAD means. Three forces, working blind, arriving at one vision.
