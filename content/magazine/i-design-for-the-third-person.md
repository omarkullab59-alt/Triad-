Mara Solenne works out of a Paris apartment stacked floor to ceiling with garments nobody else wanted. We met on a Tuesday morning. She had already been to two markets.

## TRIAD: You talk about a “third person”. Who is that?

**Mara Solenne:** When you dress someone, there are always three people involved. The person wearing it, the person looking at it — and the person who made it, who is usually absent. Often dead, sometimes unknown. I try to design for that third person. To give them a voice again.

## So the archive is a collaborator.

**MS:** Completely. I don't see vintage as a style. I see it as a conversation with people I will never meet. A coat from 1962 has opinions. My job is to listen to them and then disagree a little.

> A coat from 1962 has opinions. My job is to listen, then disagree a little.

![Rails of archival garments in an apartment](https://picsum.photos/seed/triad-mara-fig1/1800/1200?grayscale "The apartment. Every rail is a decade.")

## What do you look for at a market?

**MS:** Damage. Repairs especially. A patch tells you someone loved a garment enough to save it. That's the most valuable thing a piece of clothing can carry.

## And what don't you want?

**MS:** Anything that looks like it is trying to be photographed.
