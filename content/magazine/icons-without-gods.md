Walk through any underground club, any independent fashion fair, any art school degree show, and you will find them: halos, serpents, winged creatures, crosses distorted until they are almost geometry.

## Borrowed power

Symbols outlive the systems that made them. A dragon carved into a temple door once meant protection, threat, divine order. Printed on a hoodie today it means something looser — power without a source, belonging without a doctrine.

> We wear the icons because they still work, even after the gods have left. — Lior Baptiste

That looseness is not emptiness. It is an opening. Young creatives use these images to build private mythologies: personal gods for scenes that have no institutions.

![Stone relief with three figures](https://picsum.photos/seed/triad-icons-fig1/1800/1200?grayscale "Three figures. Origin unknown.")

## Why three

The number three appears everywhere in this vocabulary: trinities, triple goddesses, three-headed guardians at the gates of the underworld. It is the smallest number that makes a system — two is a mirror, three is a world.

It is also why this publication carries the name it does. TRIAD is not a religion. It is a reminder that every culture worth belonging to is held together by more than one force.
